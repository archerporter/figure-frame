Figure and Frame

Overview

Figure and Frame is a movement analysis tool designed for researchers studying how dancers adapt choreography to the constraints of portrait-frame video. It reads pose landmark data captured with PlatformPose and computes per-video metrics aimed at the portrait-frame compensation hypothesis: that dancers in short-form, vertically-framed video compensate for restricted horizontal space by increasing distal joint movement, direction changes, and vertical level changes to maintain dynamism.

Research Hypothesis

Portrait-frame platforms — TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts — constrain dancers to a vertical aspect ratio that limits lateral movement across the frame. The hypothesis is that dancers negotiate this constraint through three observable compensatory strategies:

  1. Increased distal articulation. When whole-body lateral travel is restricted, peripheral joints (wrists, ankles, hands, feet) carry more expressive weight. The distal/proximal velocity ratio captures this: values greater than 1× indicate that peripheral joints are moving faster than the body core.
  2. Frequent direction reversals. Rather than travelling broadly across the frame, dancers make movement dynamic through rapid changes of horizontal direction. The x-axis direction change rate counts sign reversals per second in the center-of-mass horizontal velocity.
  3. Vertical level changes. Rising and falling — plié, relevé, jump, descent — exploit the vertical dimension that portrait framing preserves. The level change rate counts local extrema per second in center-of-mass height above the estimated floor.

Metrics

Metric Landmarks used Definition
Distal / Proximal ratio Wrists (15, 16), ankles (27, 28) vs. shoulders (11, 12), hips (23, 24) Median across frames of mean distal speed ÷ mean proximal speed. Values >1 indicate more peripheral than core movement.
X direction changes /s Center of mass (centroid of shoulders + hips) Sign reversals per second in horizontal CoM velocity. Reversals only counted when |vx| ≥ 0.08 normalized units/s to suppress noise from small postural shifts.
Level changes /s Center of mass Local minima and maxima per second in CoM height above the estimated floor. Floor is the 99th percentile of maximum foot-landmark y across all frames.

All coordinates are MediaPipe normalized values (0–1 relative to frame dimensions). Velocities are in frame-widths or frame-heights per second, not metric units, and are therefore comparable only within a project whose videos share a consistent capture region.

Relationship to PlatformPose

Figure and Frame is a companion to PlatformPose, which handles capture, storage, and skeleton visualization. Figure and Frame reads the same .db files PlatformPose produces and adds no new data — it only interprets what is already there. The two tools are designed to be used together: capture and quality-check in PlatformPose, then analyze in Figure and Frame.

Any video analysis page in Figure and Frame includes a direct link to that video's skeleton in PlatformPose, so you can move between interpretive metrics and the underlying movement data without switching contexts.

Author

L. Archer Porter is a researcher working at the intersection of digital humanities, performance studies, and computational movement analysis. Figure and Frame was developed as part of ongoing research into vernacular dance on short-form video platforms.
